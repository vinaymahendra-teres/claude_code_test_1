# Stream 3 — Operations Strategy (Google Sheets — Phase 2)

> _Last updated: 2026-05-24_
> _Status: Phase 2a complete (skeleton + build script). Phase 2b (Apps Scripts) follows separately._
> _Operators: Swetha (S) — production; Shreya (Sh) — visibility._

This document is **canonical** for the Operations module's Phase 2a scope. It defines the production and procurement data layer — recipes, ingredient stock, bake scheduling, equipment maintenance, vendor master, and purchase orders.

---

## 1. Why Operations belongs in its own file

The Finance module (`03_Finance.xlsx`) tracks money flowing in and out. Operations tracks what Swetha makes and what she buys to make it. These two concerns have different edit patterns, different row growth rates, and different primary operators.

**Production data grows faster than financial data.** At 8–10 orders per week:
- `Recipes` accumulates rows as new products are added (low velocity — perhaps 2–3 new products per month, but each has 5–12 BOM rows).
- `Ingredients` stays bounded at ~20–30 rows indefinitely — it's a master list, not a ledger.
- `Bake Plan` grows at order volume: ~8–10 rows per week, ~400 rows per year. It is also the tab most frequently written by Apps Script (nightly), making it the highest-churn tab in the entire system.
- `Purchase Orders` grows at ~2–4 POs per week: ~150–200 rows per year.

Keeping Operations separate from Finance also keeps file sizes manageable for Google Sheets' mobile editor, which Swetha uses from the kitchen.

The **procurement loop** is the critical data dependency: Ingredients → PAR breach → Purchase Order → Money Out (in Finance). PO IDs are cross-referenced in Finance's Money Out `Linked PO ID` column. This is the only inter-file join in the Operations layer; everything else is self-contained.

## 2. Two halves: Production and Procurement

### Production (tabs 1–4)

**Recipes** is the bill-of-materials (BOM) layer. Every product Swetha sells has a BOM: one row per ingredient, with quantities, units, and unit costs. The `Line cost` (quantity × unit cost) per ingredient sums to the **total ingredient cost per batch** — the primary input to pricing decisions (Stream 4).

**Ingredients** is the stock-tracking layer. It is a master list, not a ledger. Each row represents one SKU in the pantry. Current stock, PAR level, and reorder quantity drive the PAR breach logic: `IF(Current stock < PAR, "YES", "")`. The Apps Script `par_breach_alerter.gs` reads this formula and emails Shreya every morning when PAR breaches exist.

**Bake Plan** is the production queue. It is the only tab in the system that is **fully written by an Apps Script** (`bake_plan_generator.gs`). Swetha does not enter data here — she reads it. The script runs nightly at 23:00 IST, pulls Confirmed + In Production orders from `01_CRM_Sales`, computes bake start dates by working backwards from delivery dates, and writes the result as the next day's production schedule.

**Equipment Maintenance** tracks the service schedule for kitchen equipment. Formulaic status flags (OK / Schedule now / OVERDUE) give Swetha a one-glance health check. At cottage scale, equipment failure is an operational tail risk — a broken OTG means zero production for days. This tab costs 5 minutes per quarter to maintain; the downside of not maintaining it is uncapped.

### Procurement (tabs 5–6)

**Vendors** is a lightweight master of every supplier used for ingredients, packaging, and services. It is simpler than the Finance Vendor Ledger (which aggregates spend). The Operations Vendors tab adds procurement-specific fields: lead time and MOQ, which determine when to raise POs and for how much.

**Purchase Orders** logs every procurement event. Each PO is raised when a PAR breach is detected (either manually by Shreya or auto-drafted by `par_breach_alerter.gs`). POs link back to Finance Money Out via `Linked Money Out ID` once payment is made. The auto-ID (`PO-0001`, `PO-0002`, …) is generated by a formula and matches the cross-reference already used in Finance's Money Out sample data.

## 3. Tab schemas

### 3.1 `Recipes`

One row per ingredient per product (BOM rows). Multiple rows share the same product.

| Col | Header (as shown in sheet) | Type | Source / formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Recipe ID** | text | `=IF(B2="","","REC-"&TEXT(ROW()-1,"0000"))` — auto; unique per row |
| B | **Product** | text | list: Cake (1kg), Cake (2kg), Cupcakes (1 dozen), Brownies (12), Cake Tub (6), Bomboloni (6) |
| C | **Ingredient** | text | manual — ingredient name (must match Ingredients tab for traceability) |
| D | **Quantity** | number | manual — amount per batch |
| E | **Unit** | text | list: g, kg, ml, L, each |
| F | **Unit cost (₹)** | number | manual — cost per single unit (per gram, per ml, per each) |
| G | **Line cost (₹)** | number | `=IFERROR(D2*F2,"")` — quantity × unit cost; derived |
| H | **Notes** | text | free — vendor, brand, substitution notes |

To compute total ingredient cost per batch: `SUMIF(Recipes!B:B, "Cake (2kg)", Recipes!G:G)`. This formula pattern is how the pricing workbook (Stream 4) will pull cost data.

### 3.2 `Ingredients`

Master stock list. One row per ingredient SKU. Not a ledger — does not log individual consumption events at this scale.

| Col | Header (as shown in sheet) | Type | Source / formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Ingredient ID** | text | `=IF(B2="","","ING-"&TEXT(ROW()-1,"0000"))` |
| B | **Ingredient name** | text | manual |
| C | **Category** | text | list: Flour, Sugar, Dairy, Fat, Egg, Flavour, Colour, Specialty, Packaging |
| D | **Unit** | text | list: g, kg, ml, L, each |
| E | **Current stock** | number | manual — operator updates after each use and each delivery |
| F | **PAR (reorder when below)** | number | manual — minimum working stock; set once, reviewed quarterly |
| G | **Reorder qty** | number | manual — how much to order when PAR is breached |
| H | **Preferred vendor** | text | manual — must match Vendors tab for cross-reference |
| I | **Avg cost per unit (₹)** | number | manual — cost per gram/ml/each; updated when prices change |
| J | **Days cover at typical use** | number | manual — estimated days current stock lasts at typical weekly usage |
| K | **Last reorder date** | date | manual — updated when PO is raised |
| L | **Reorder due?** | text | `=IF(E2<F2,"YES","")` — derived; YES when current stock < PAR |
| M | **Notes** | text | free |

The `Reorder due?` column (L) is the primary input to `par_breach_alerter.gs`.

### 3.3 `Bake Plan`

Production queue. Populated entirely by `bake_plan_generator.gs` — do not edit manually. The tab ships empty; the Apps Script fills it nightly.

| Col | Header (as shown in sheet) | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Delivery date** | date | Script — from CRM order |
| B | **Order ID** | text | Script — from CRM order |
| C | **Customer** | text | Script — from CRM order |
| D | **Product** | text | Script — from CRM order |
| E | **Quantity** | number | Script — from CRM order |
| F | **Customisation** | text | Script — from CRM order (flavour, message, design brief) |
| G | **Production status** | text | list: Queued, Prep, Bake, Decorate, Done. Script seeds as "Queued"; Swetha updates manually as work progresses |
| H | **Bake date (when to start)** | date | Script — delivery date minus lead time per product (configured in script) |
| I | **Linked Recipe ID** | text | Script — matched via product name lookup against Recipes tab |

The placeholder row shipped in the xlsx (`(populated by bake_plan_generator.gs — nightly 23:00 IST)`) is overwritten on first script run.

### 3.4 `Equipment Maintenance`

Service schedule for kitchen equipment. One row per piece of equipment.

| Col | Header (as shown in sheet) | Type | Source / formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Equipment** | text | list: OTG, Stand Mixer, Microwave-Convection, Food Processor, Gas Hob, Chimney, Kettle, Other |

| H | **Cost last service (₹)** | number | manual |
| I | **Notes** | text | free — what was serviced, parts replaced |

### 3.6 `Purchase Orders`

One row per procurement event. Raised when a PAR breach is detected or when stock needs to be topped up ahead of a large order.

| Col | Header (as shown in sheet) | Type | Source / formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **PO ID** | text | `=IF(B2="","","PO-"&TEXT(ROW()-1,"0000"))` — auto |
| B | **Vendor** | text | manual — must match Vendors tab |
| C | **Raised on** | date | manual |
| D | **Expected delivery** | date | manual — raised on + vendor lead time |
| E | **Items (summary)** | text | manual — free-text summary (e.g. "Maida 25kg + Castor sugar 15kg") |
| F | **Total amount (₹)** | number | manual |
| G | **Status** | text | list: Drafted, Sent, Acknowledged, Received, Paid, Cancelled |
| H | **Linked Money Out ID** | text | manual — cross-reference to Finance Money Out (e.g. "MOUT-0001"); blank until paid |
| I | **Notes** | text | free |

PO IDs are the primary cross-reference between Operations and Finance. When Shreya pays a vendor in Finance's Money Out tab, she enters the PO ID in `Money Out.Linked PO ID` and the Money Out ID in `Purchase Orders.Linked Money Out ID`. This gives both files a bidirectional join without any automation.

## 4. Operator workflow (steady state)

### Swetha (S) — Production

**Once (setup):**
1. Enter all current recipe BOMs in the Recipes tab — one row per ingredient per product.
2. Enter initial stock levels in the Ingredients tab. Set PAR levels conservatively (err high; you can lower them once you know your rhythm).
3. Confirm equipment list in Equipment Maintenance; fill in last service dates from memory or receipts.

**Each morning:**
1. Open the Bake Plan tab. Review today's production queue (sorted by Bake date).
2. Resolve any equipment Status = "Schedule now" or "OVERDUE" rows in Equipment Maintenance — book the service before it becomes urgent.
3. As work progresses, update Production status in Bake Plan (Queued → Prep → Bake → Decorate → Done).

**As stock is used:**
1. Update Current stock in the Ingredients tab after each bake. This is the only manual stock-keeping step; ingredient auto-deduction from Bake Plan is out of scope at this volume (see §6).
2. When a new bag of flour is opened: update Last reorder date when the reorder arrives.

### Shreya (Sh) — Visibility + Procurement

**Each morning:**
1. Check the PAR breach email from `par_breach_alerter.gs` (Phase 2b).
2. For each YES row in Ingredients.Reorder due?: raise a PO in Purchase Orders — draft the row, fill in vendor, items, expected amount.
3. Send the PO (verbally, WhatsApp, or email depending on vendor) and update Status to "Sent".

**When delivery arrives:**
1. Update Purchase Orders.Status to "Received".
2. Update Ingredients.Current stock with the new quantity.
3. Update Ingredients.Last reorder date.

**When payment is made:**
1. Log the outflow in Finance Money Out tab with Linked PO ID.
2. Update Purchase Orders.Status to "Paid" and fill in Linked Money Out ID.

## 5. Apps Scripts (Phase 2b)

Full source code lands in `operations_sheets/apps_script/` in Phase 2b. Descriptions below establish the contract.

### `bake_plan_generator.gs`

**Trigger:** Time-driven, daily 23:00 IST.

**What it does:**
1. Opens `01_CRM_Sales` (via `CRM_SPREADSHEET_ID` constant) and reads all orders with Status = "Confirmed" or "In Production" and Delivery date within the next 14 days.
2. For each order, computes the Bake date by subtracting a configurable lead time per product (default: 1 day for cakes, 1 day for cupcakes/bomboloni; decorator products may need 2 days if multi-tier).
3. Clears the Bake Plan tab below the header row, then writes the full production queue sorted by Bake date ascending.
4. Looks up the Linked Recipe ID from the Recipes tab by matching the order's product name.
5. Seeds Production status as "Queued" for all new rows; does not overwrite existing statuses on rows already present (so Swetha's in-progress updates are preserved).

**Configuration constants at top of script:**
- `CRM_SPREADSHEET_ID`
- `OPS_SPREADSHEET_ID`
- `LEAD_TIME_BY_PRODUCT` — map of product names to lead time in days

### `par_breach_alerter.gs`

**Trigger:** Time-driven, daily 09:00 IST.

**What it does:**
1. Reads the Ingredients tab. Finds all rows where `Reorder due?` (col L) = "YES".
2. For each breached ingredient, computes suggested PO line: vendor (from Preferred vendor), reorder qty, and estimated cost (Avg cost per unit × Reorder qty).
3. Drafts a row in the Purchase Orders tab for each breach not already covered by an open PO (Status = Drafted / Sent / Acknowledged / Received for the same vendor within the last 7 days).
4. Sends an email to `RECIPIENT` (Shreya) with a summary table: ingredient, current stock, PAR level, suggested order, vendor.

**Configuration constants:**
- `OPS_SPREADSHEET_ID`
- `RECIPIENT`
- `BAKERY_BRAND`

## 6. Out of scope for Phase 2

| Excluded | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Ingredient auto-deduction from production | Manual stock updates suffice at ≤10 orders/week. Auto-deduction requires confirmed Bake Plan rows to map exactly to recipe BOMs — adds fragility without meaningful time saving at this scale. |
| FIFO / FEFO lot tracking | Cottage production uses ingredients within days of purchase. Lot-level tracking is a food-manufacturing concern that activates above ₹50L turnover or when multiple storage locations are used. |
| Multi-location inventory | Single home kitchen in Hyderabad. No staging area, no cold store, no satellite location. |
| Supplier invoice management | Invoices are photographed and stored in Google Drive; linking Drive files to POs is a Phase 3 improvement. |
| Automated stock reconciliation (physical vs system) | Swetha counts stock by eye; at these volumes a weekly or fortnightly count takes 10 minutes. A full cycle-count system is overkill. |

## 7. Open dependencies on other Streams

| From Stream | What it sharpens |
|---|---|
| Stream 1 — Capacity (oven sizing) | Oven capacity sets the maximum daily bake volume, which constrains how many orders `bake_plan_generator.gs` can schedule per day. Until Stream 1 finalises the oven spec, the script assumes a single OTG + a planned second oven. |
| Stream 3 — Entity / vendor account openings | Formal vendor accounts (e.g., Metro HoReCa credit terms, Callebaut distributor account) precede any PO discipline beyond ad-hoc cash purchases. Vendor tab is seeded assuming walk-in / cash/UPI relationships. |
| Stream 4 — Pricing | Pricing depends on `Recipes.Line cost` roll-up: `SUMIF(Recipes!B:B, product, Recipes!G:G)` gives total ingredient cost per batch. Pricing model cannot be finalised until BOMs are entered and unit costs are confirmed. |

Phase 2a does **not** block on any of these. The schema absorbs them as they land.
